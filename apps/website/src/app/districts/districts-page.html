Ridings, wards and districts

Every other tool assumes your race looks like theirs.

A Canadian tool asks for your riding and has never heard of a state house district. An American one asks for your ward and breaks on a federal riding. Neither one has a box for a US Senate race, which has no district at all.

pplCRM asks one question when you set up your campaign: what are you running for? Every screen after that speaks the language of the answer.

Some seats have no district at all

An at-large seat is elected across a whole state or a whole city. pplCRM treats that as a real answer, not a blank field: the seat-name question simply stops being asked, and your organizing runs on the precincts or polls underneath.

A door sits inside every line at once

One American address is inside a congressional district and a state senate district and a state house district and a council district and a precinct, all at the same time. pplCRM stores every one of them against the household, so no level overwrites another and last year's map can sit beside this year's.

That is also why we never guess meaning from a label. In Massachusetts cities a ward is not a seat at all: it is a voting subdivision holding precincts, while Boston's council districts are different lines again. The same word, two different jobs, both stored correctly.

The region can override the word

Alberta and Saskatchewan say constituency. Newfoundland and Prince Edward Island say district. Quebec says circonscription. New York calls a precinct an election district. You choose the province or state; the word comes with it.

And you can override the word

If your race uses a word we did not predict, type your own and every screen uses it instead. A default that cannot be corrected is just a different kind of wrong.

Your turfs follow the same lines

Turf cutting uses the finest boundary your workspace holds for that race, so a walk list stops at the polling division or the precinct rather than sprawling across two of them.

Four ways to get a map

Draw your own.

A boundary map reaches pplCRM in exactly four ways, and these are all of them: select a published map if your seat is a Canadian federal riding, an Ontario or Alberta provincial riding, or a US congressional, state senate or state house district; import the district columns your voter file already carries; upload a GeoJSON from your municipality's open data portal; or draw the areas yourself. No map of your city council's wards is included — that one you bring or you make.

Drawing is the one that matters most. Thousands of municipalities in both countries publish nothing you can use, or publish a PDF, and hunting for a shapefile is not a plan.

No geocoding on the way in

A district column is a fact the file already carries, so importing it needs no address lookup at all. The rows land with their geography attached, ready to filter, list, count and export immediately. Cutting turfs additionally needs each door located on the map — that lookup is the metered, plan-gated part, spread over a daily budget. Which columns are recognized.

Already have the shapes?

Upload a GeoJSON of your ward or council map, name the set, and say which property in the file holds each area's name. It becomes a normal set afterwards: you can rename an area, reshape it, or delete one that came over wrong. Files run to 20 MB and 5,000 areas per set, and a file over a limit is refused with a message naming which one. The full boundary guide.

Four ways in, one result

Selected, imported, uploaded or hand-drawn, an area behaves the same everywhere afterwards: a column in the grid, a filter in a smart list, a boundary for turf cutting, and a heading in the coverage report.

“Is this door in my riding?”

Once a map is in place, every located address is placed in its area automatically — no tagging, no spreadsheet lookup — and each household says whether it is in your riding or ward, in a neighbouring one, or outside the map altogether. A seat elected by more than one ward counts every one of them, which is what a regional councillor needs. Matching costs nothing and calls no paid service: it is arithmetic on coordinates already on file.
